<p class="lede">The documents are there. The story between them is missing.</p>

A project can have a thorough README, plenty of tickets, architecture notes,
and a long chat history—and still leave a new contributor guessing.
Which proposal became a decision? Does the specification describe today’s
system? Why was an apparently simpler approach rejected?

<Explainer kind="problem" />

## The gap between documents

Imagine a team adding an export feature. A conversation proposes exporting
everything. A later review limits the export to information the user owns.
The code changes, but the first proposal is still easy to find.

Months later, a developer or an AI assistant reads that proposal and treats it
as the requirement. The words were preserved; their authority and relationship
to the current implementation were not clear enough.

This is the problem NKF is designed to address: **project knowledge that is
available but hard to trust in context.**

## Make the distinctions visible

NKF gives different kinds of knowledge distinct roles:

- **Intent:** the outcome a piece of work is trying to deliver.
- **Proposal:** a possible direction, with alternatives and trade-offs.
- **Decision:** the chosen direction and its reasons.
- **Specification:** the accepted meaning and requirements.
- **Realization:** how that meaning maps to the implementation.
- **Validation:** what checks found about a particular snapshot.

Explicit identities, relationships, and revision bindings connect these roles.
Readers can follow the reasoning instead of guessing from filenames or the
date of the last edit.

## Keep the next change grounded

For the export feature, a contributor can follow the selected decision to the
requirement it establishes and then to the implementation that claims to meet
it. A future change can preserve that rationale or deliberately replace it.

The checker helps detect supported structural and consistency problems. People
still review whether the account is true and whether a proposed change should
be accepted. That responsibility is essential to making the knowledge useful.
