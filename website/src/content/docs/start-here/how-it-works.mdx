<p class="lede">Write the meaning. Make its relationships explicit. Review the change.</p>

Consider a team adding an export feature. It needs to preserve what the feature
is for, what was decided, and how the resulting system meets the requirement.
NKF gives each part of that account a place and a role.

<Explainer kind="flow" />

## Follow one change

1. **State the intent in a Task.** People need a way to download information
   they own. Record the intended outcome, constraints, and acceptance criteria.
2. **Explore the direction in a Design.** Compare approaches, describe
   trade-offs, and keep the proposed direction visibly distinct from a decision.
3. **Record the chosen direction in a Decision.** The responsible authority
   chooses the approach and records why. Acceptance concerns exact revisions;
   a proposal does not become normative merely because it was discussed.
4. **Express the requirement in a Specification.** Define which information
   the export includes and the behavior the feature must provide.
5. **Map it to implementation in a Realization.** Connect those requirements
   to the relevant code, interfaces, and evidence. Keep the current account
   aligned with what has actually been implemented and confirmed.
6. **Validate and review the snapshot.** Run the supported NKF checks, review
   the meaning, and use appropriate software tests and observations to assess
   the implementation.

This example explains the roles. It is not a new export policy or a claim that
every change needs an identical sequence of new documents.

## Readable meaning, explicit declarations

The human-readable source is Markdown. Alongside it, YAML declarations identify
the knowledge bundle, its documents and records, and the relationships and
revision bindings the adopted contract requires.

These declarations give tools something precise to check. They support the
written meaning; they cannot silently redefine it. A profile provides the
requirements for a supported kind of root, such as a Product or Technology.

## Four questions that stay separate

| Question | What answers it |
| --- | --- |
| Has this exact meaning been accepted? | The responsible authority’s acceptance and its provenance. |
| Is this implementation account confirmed? | The applicable Realization confirmation and its exact scope. |
| Does this snapshot conform to the adopted NKF contract? | Validation against the supported contract and declared inputs. |
| Is the feature deployed and working? | The operational system, tests, and relevant runtime observations. |

A successful check answers the third question within its scope. It cannot
supply the other three answers.

## Keep change deliberate

When requirements evolve, preserve accepted history and make the successor
meaning explicit. When implementation changes, update the current account and
review the affected relationships. An existing consumer keeps its selected
release until its authority deliberately approves an upgrade.

That is the central habit NKF supports: keep the reason, the accepted meaning,
and the implementation connected as the work changes.
