NKF is useful when several people—or people and AI assistants—need to work
from the same understanding without losing who has the authority to decide.

<Explainer kind="people" />

## People deciding what should be built

Product owners and other decision makers can separate an idea from an accepted
requirement. They can preserve why a direction was chosen and make the boundary
of that decision clear to the people implementing it.

NKF records the authority a repository declares. It does not assign that
authority or take the acceptance decision away from its owner.

## People building and maintaining systems

Engineers and maintainers can connect implementation details to the requirements
they serve. That gives reviews, handovers, and later changes a route back to the
reasoning rather than relying entirely on the memory of the original author.

The current-system Realization is the starting point for the implementation
account. Code, tests, and runtime observations remain necessary evidence.

## People joining or reviewing a project

A newcomer can start with the current account, follow the relevant decisions,
and read older proposals as history. A reviewer can ask whether a change updates
the meaning, the implementation, or both, and look for the corresponding evidence.

The benefit depends on the team keeping the account accurate. NKF makes its
structure explicit; it cannot make neglected prose current by itself.

## Teams working with AI assistants

AI assistants benefit from readable knowledge with explicit roles and boundaries.
An assistant can distinguish a proposal from an accepted requirement, find the
current implementation account, and run the same supported validation command
as another contributor.

This reduces ambiguity in the material an assistant receives. It does not
guarantee correct reasoning or safe execution, and it does not let the assistant
accept knowledge merely because a check passes.
